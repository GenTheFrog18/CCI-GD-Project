# Delvers of the Abyss — Narrative Master Reference

> Working narrative reference for dialogue writing. This consolidates the current GDD, answered design decisions, roadmap story scope, and authored dialogue resources. It does not replace the English GDD as design authority. Anything marked `TBD` is not established canon.

## 1. The Story in One Paragraph

`PLAYER_NAME` is a curious young girl who descends into the Abyss to understand its mysteries and search for her missing parents. She is not a conventional warrior. She survives by preparing routes, studying creatures, experimenting with relics, and deciding what is worth carrying home. The descent is only half of the expedition: the player must also plan an ascent through the Curse, return with useful discoveries, and eventually reach the Layer 3 entrance.

The jam build ends at the Layer 3 entrance. Layer 3 itself is not playable.

## 2. Narrative Tone and Design Rules

- The setting is original fantasy adventure; it is not a direct adaptation of the inspirations listed in the GDD.
- Curiosity, preparation, experimentation, and readable consequences are the central emotional goals.
- Dialogue should be short and player-advanced. Gameplay-critical information should also be communicated visually.
- The player should feel observant and resourceful, not helpless or needlessly punished.
- Creatures are part of an ecosystem. They are threats, obstacles, or sources of opportunity rather than disposable combat targets.
- Relics should invite questions: what are they, what do they do, and what does using them reveal?
- Do not state unresolved lore as fact. Use `TBD` placeholders until the lead supplies canon.
- The current written dialogue is Indonesian. New authored dialogue should establish whether the final script remains Indonesian or is translated consistently.

## 3. Protagonist

### Current canon

- Name: `PLAYER_NAME` / current authored dialogue uses **Elenara**.
- Gender/presentation: girl.
- Motivation: discover the Abyss's mysteries and search for her missing parents.
- Personality currently shown in dialogue: curious, persistent, self-aware, impatient with being treated as a child, and willing to challenge warnings.
- She has heard stories about the Abyss since childhood.
- An older man has repeatedly told her that she asks too many questions and that she is not ready.

### Still unresolved

- Exact age.
- Formal training and expedition experience.
- Her parents' names, profession, disappearance, and relationship to the Abyss.
- Her relationship to the Surface community.
- Whether `Elenara` is the final name or only the current placeholder.
- Final dialogue style, vocabulary, humor level, and emotional range.
- What specifically makes her start this expedition now.

### Writing direction

Elenara's voice should turn curiosity into action. She notices details, makes hypotheses, and sometimes underestimates danger. Her arc should move from “I want answers” toward “I understand that answers have a cost, and I still choose how to proceed.”

## 4. The Abyss and the World

The Abyss is a dangerous deep environment beneath the Surface. Its exact nature, origin, inhabitants, and reason for existing remain `TBD`. People descend despite the danger because it contains mysteries, relics, resources, and answers unavailable on the Surface.

### Surface

The Surface is the preparation and recovery hub. It contains:

- a shop and supply services;
- information and item-knowledge support;
- replacement services for essential equipment;
- entrances to the east and west Layer 1 routes;
- the older man/training relationship currently implied by the opening dialogue;
- the Layer 1 gate and senior diver.

The shopkeeper's identity, relationship with Elenara, and exact explanation of buying, selling, delivery, and replacement are `TBD`.

### Layer 1

Layer 1 is a bright green landscape of cliffs and meadows with dim caves. Its two routes teach the player's first systemic lessons:

- Rope preparation and safe return planning;
- sight and sound manipulation;
- creatures reacting to agitation, noise, force, and items;
- relic experimentation;
- the first Ascension Curse package.

The west route is more open and emphasizes knockback/flying threats. The east route is more compact and emphasizes numerous small threats and hazards.

### Layer 1 gate

The senior diver guards access deeper into the expedition. The Blue Whistle is the reliable credential for passage, but the player may also be able to distract, bypass, or defeat the guard through systemic play. The guard's identity, history, and exact reason for accepting or enforcing the whistle rule are `TBD`.

### Layer 2

Layer 2 begins as an inverted forest traversed through upside-down canopy. The middle contains an outpost/shop and dangerous forest. The lower area becomes a monster gauntlet before the Layer 3 entrance.

The optional Layer 2 quest authority asks for one quest relic and gives two separate rewards:

- the Moon Whistle, which is the progression credential/ending reward;
- a separate powerful relic that helps with the gauntlet.

The quest is optional and does not open a hard gate. The authority's identity, relationship to the Abyss, reason for holding the rewards, quest relic, powerful relic, and exact dialogue are `TBD`.

### Layer 3 entrance and ending

The player reaches the Layer 3 entrance after the Layer 2 gauntlet. Interacting with it ends the jam build without checking for an additional requirement. The ending should confirm that the player has reached the next mystery rather than pretending that Layer 3 has already been explored.

Exact ending sequence, final reveal, and closing message are `TBD`.

## 5. Narrative Progression

### Opening / New Game

1. A short prologue establishes that the Abyss is old, deep, and dangerous.
2. The player is identified as a Delver or prospective Delver.
3. The game begins at the Surface with basic equipment and the Red Whistle.
4. The Surface introduction shows Elenara approaching Layer 1 and thinking about the stories she has heard.
5. Her immediate goal is to meet the older man at the training area.

### First Surface cycle

The player prepares, shops, learns the basic interaction language, and chooses an east or west route. The Surface should make the expedition feel possible while implying that preparation matters.

### First descent

The player encounters authored terrain, loose items, relics, creatures, and the first consequences of carrying too much or descending without a return plan. Dialogue should explain only what the player needs; discoveries should carry some of the narrative load.

### Return and progression

The player returns with items and knowledge, sells suitable discoveries, and works toward the Blue Whistle delivery threshold. The fiction for why the threshold grants the Blue Whistle is `TBD`.

### Layer 1 gate

The senior diver interaction is the first explicit social gate. It should communicate the Blue Whistle rule and leave room for a systemic solution when the player lacks the credential.

### Layer 2 midpoint

The shop/outpost greeting establishes that the deeper environment is different from Layer 1 and provides a moment of safety before the gauntlet.

### Layer 2 ending

The final gatekeeper interaction, optional quest reward, and Moon Whistle sequence lead into the Layer 3 entrance and closing screen.

### Death and continuation

Death ends the living run and removes its inventory/world state. Learned relic knowledge survives. The exact in-world explanation for why knowledge persists while the run does not is `TBD`. Death feedback should show newly learned relic descriptions rather than treating death as a narrative reset of the protagonist's memory.

## 6. Character and Encounter Writing Hooks

These are gameplay identities that dialogue can reinforce. They are not necessarily speaking characters.

### Old Man / The Wanderer

The current Old Man resource presents the speaker as **The Wanderer**. The current Surface introduction calls him “the old man” and says he is at the training area. This suggests a mentor-like figure who knows more about the Abyss than he tells Elenara.

Established traits:

- he has repeatedly told Elenara she asks too many questions;
- he answers questions about the Abyss with “not yet” or equivalent refusal;
- he may be deliberately withholding information rather than simply lacking it;
- his current relationship is familiar enough for Elenara to complain about hearing the same lecture again.

His real name, history, motive for withholding information, and connection to Elenara's parents are `TBD`.

### Shadow

Shadow currently uses the same authored exchange as The Wanderer. No distinct canon has been written yet. Treat Shadow as a placeholder until a separate role, identity, and purpose are decided.

### Shopkeeper

The Surface shopkeeper is a functional information point for supplies, selling, delivery, and replacements. Character identity and relationship to Elenara are `TBD`. Dialogue can explain the practical rules without claiming lore that has not been approved.

### Senior Diver

The senior diver is a Layer 1 gate guardian. The character recognizes the Blue Whistle and represents the social rule separating the Surface/Layer 1 expedition from deeper access. The character is not currently designed as a traditional boss.

### Layer 2 quest authority

An optional authority at the Layer 2 shop holds the quest relic exchange. Their role is to offer the safest route through the lower gauntlet, not to force a single solution. Identity and motivation are `TBD`.

### Creatures

Creature writing should describe behavior through signs, reactions, and consequences rather than humanizing every enemy:

- Frogs prefer loose items and may steal one item from the player.
- Knockback birds defend territory and punish unsafe positioning.
- Thorn Blooms are stationary hazards that release bleeding needles when agitated.
- Lantern Snails are luminous neutral hazards that can dazzle visible actors and drop a Lantern Crystal when killed.
- Cave Spiders are sound-sensitive crawlers whose projectile slows, poisons, and marks the player for the flyer.
- Large Flyers are open-air threats that patrol, pursue, and commit to deadly dives.

All enemies are killable, but combat is costly and optional. Creature encounters should support avoidance, manipulation, and item-based solutions.

## 7. Relics as Narrative

Relics are not only equipment. They are evidence about how the Abyss works. The player may initially know what an item looks like but not its complete purpose. Successful signature uses increase persistent knowledge, and the Info Book can reveal descriptions.

Current narrative-relevant relic hooks:

- **Red Whistle:** starting whistle and current Surface identity.
- **Blue Whistle:** delivery progression and Layer 1 gate credential.
- **Moon Whistle:** optional Layer 2 quest reward and ending credential.
- **Rope:** preparation, route planning, and return safety.
- **Lantern Crystal:** light/flash utility connected to Lantern Snails.
- **Hushcap:** suppresses enemy sight updates while the player is inside its cloud.
- **Cling Resin:** slows loose-world-item movement and creates environmental control.
- **Driftseed:** changes how certain enemy categories can be controlled.
- **Silver Weight:** heavy force and impact; its damaged states communicate that powerful tools have costs.
- **Sun Sphere:** an activated thrown object that retains momentum and remains in the world.
- **Numbing Pill:** temporary relief from the Ascension Curse.

Exact origins, naming conventions, cultural meaning, and why knowledge is hidden are `TBD`.

## 8. Ascension Curse as Story Pressure

The Curse makes upward travel a pacing and planning problem. The player descends to find answers and resources, but returning is not free. Layer 1 and Layer 2 use different temporary effects, so the player learns that depth changes the rules.

Dialogue should not present the Curse as random punishment. It should frame it as a known danger that experienced Delvers prepare for, observe, and counter with timing, safe zones, and Numbing Pills.

The exact in-world origin, public knowledge, symptoms, and long-term consequence of the Curse are `TBD`.

## 12. Authoring Checklist

For each new sequence, record:

- Sequence ID and interaction state (`first`, `repeat`, `completed`).
- Speaker and portrait.
- Player-facing purpose: what the player should understand or feel.
- Lines in order, with no gameplay-critical rule hidden in flavor text only.
- Any required item/flag condition.
- Any item exchange or progression action.
- What happens if the player leaves early.
- Whether the sequence should repeat on the next interaction.
- Whether the text introduces canon or remains intentionally uncertain.
